# Initial Analytical Questions

## AQ1 — Service Demand

Which service problems generate the greatest request volume?

## AQ2 — Backlog

Which service problems contribute disproportionately to unresolved backlog?

## AQ3 — Resolution Reliability

Which service categories exhibit unusually long median, P75 and P90
resolution durations?

## AQ4 — Geographic Burden

Which boroughs, ZIP codes or community boards experience persistent
service-request demand?

## AQ5 — Service × Geography Interaction

Are particular service problems repeatedly concentrated within specific
locations?

## AQ6 — Agency-Service Operations

Within comparable service categories, where do agency/service combinations
show unusually high backlog or resolution durations?

## AQ7 — Temporal Patterns

Does service demand exhibit meaningful hourly, weekday or seasonal patterns?

---

These questions may change after profiling.

No analytical question should be retained merely because it sounds impressive.
It must be supported by the source data and its business semantics.

# Day 2 Analytical Questions

## D2-AQ1 — Backlog Composition

What combinations of agency × service problem account for the largest share
of current unresolved demand?

Distinguish:

- high backlog because of high request volume
- high backlog because of unusually high unresolved rate
- high backlog because requests remain unresolved for unusually long periods

---

## D2-AQ2 — Backlog Aging

How is current backlog distributed across age cohorts?

Investigate:

< 1 day
1–3 days
3–7 days
7–14 days
14–30 days
30–90 days
90–180 days
180+ days

Identify which services contribute most to the oldest backlog.

## D2-AQ3 — Resolution-Time Structure

Do service categories exhibit distinctive resolution-time distributions?

Investigate services such as:

Food Establishment
Smoking or Vaping
Building/Use
For Hire Vehicle Complaint
Tree-related services

Determine whether apparent delays represent:

- naturally long workflows
- administrative timing patterns
- scheduled closure behaviour
- genuine long-tail operational cases

---

## D2-AQ4 — Geographic Service Hotspots

Which service problem × geography combinations repeatedly generate demand?

Use ZIP code and Community Board initially.

Do not interpret raw request volume as population-adjusted burden unless
population normalization is explicitly introduced.

---

## D2-AQ5 — Temporal Service Patterns

Which services explain:

- morning demand peaks
- nighttime activity
- weekday/weekend differences
- major high-demand days
- monthly variation

In particular, investigate the January–February high-demand days rather than
assuming their cause.

---

## D2-AQ6 — Recurrent Local Service Demand

Can repeated requests at the same or nearby location reveal persistent service
conditions?

Repeated service-request fingerprints are not automatically duplicates.

Instead, investigate whether repeated submissions can become a defensible
measure of recurring local service demand.
