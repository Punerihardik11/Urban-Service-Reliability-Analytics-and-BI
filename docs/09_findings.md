# Initial Operational Findings

## Scope

NYC-wide 311 Service Requests

Created:
2026-01-01 through 2026-08-29

Total Requests:
2,644,153

Current Backlog:
186,436

Backlog Rate:
7.05%

---

## Finding 1 — Request Volume Is Not a Reliable Measure of Operational Pressure

The five largest service problems account for 42.46% of all observed service
requests.

However, several of the highest-volume services maintain extremely small
current backlog rates.

Examples:

Illegal Parking
- Requests: 411,917
- Backlog: 281
- Backlog Rate: 0.07%

Noise - Residential
- Requests: 248,063
- Backlog: 221
- Backlog Rate: 0.09%

Blocked Driveway
- Requests: 129,406
- Backlog: 98
- Backlog Rate: 0.08%

Operational implication:

Raw demand volume alone should not determine operational prioritization.

Demand, unresolved volume and backlog persistence must be considered
separately.

## Finding 2 — Current Backlog Is Materially Aged

## Finding 4 — Resolution Behaviour Is Strongly Service-Specific

Observed resolution-time distributions differ dramatically across services.

Examples:

Food Establishment
- Median: approximately 60.0 days
- P90: approximately 60.1 days

Smoking or Vaping
- Median: approximately 56.7 days
- P90: approximately 60.0 days

Building/Use
- Median: approximately 29.9 days
- P90: approximately 111.5 days

For Hire Vehicle Complaint
- Median: approximately 21.9 days
- P90: approximately 125.7 days

The highly concentrated approximately-60-day behaviour in some services
suggests process or administrative timing effects that require further
investigation.

Resolution time must therefore not be interpreted identically across all
service categories.

## Finding 5 — Demand Has Service-Specific Geographic Concentration

Highest-volume ZIP codes include:

11226 — 43,298 requests
10452 — 37,657
10453 — 37,484
10467 — 36,104

However, the dominant service varies geographically.

Examples include:

Illegal Parking × 11101 — 12,702
Illegal Parking × 11201 — 11,211
Noise - Residential × 10456 — 7,790
HEAT/HOT WATER × 10468 — 6,840

This supports service-specific geographic hotspot analysis rather than a
single generic complaint-density map.

Raw ZIP volume must not be interpreted as population-adjusted service burden.

---

## Temporal Observation

Average observed demand was approximately 10,972 requests per day.

Demand peaks during the morning, with the largest hourly volume around
9 AM–11 AM.

Several of the largest individual daily spikes occur during January and
February.

The source does not by itself establish why those spikes occurred.

Their service composition will be investigated before assigning a cause.
